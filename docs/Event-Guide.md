# Raid Roulette: detailed event guide

Click an event to reveal its explanation. Easter-egg solutions are intentionally omitted.

## Bosses and factions

<details>
<summary>Boss Run Down</summary>

One randomly selected boss is assigned to hunt you. The hunter continually updates its route toward your position, so waiting in one area can bring the fight to you. Native navigation still matters: this is a pursuing enemy, not an opponent guaranteed to reach every player-accessible ledge.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Boss Convention</summary>

Multiple bosses and their company share one location. Expect several dangerous encounters instead of the map’s usual boss possibilities. Plan ammunition, routes and an escape before picking a fight with a gathering.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Raider Assault</summary>

Raiders replace the usual opposition. Their equipment and combat behavior make familiar routes more dangerous. Your own starting loadout stays yours, so bring something suited to a tougher raid.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Cultist Invasion</summary>

Cultists replace ordinary encounters and bring their characteristic equipment and close-range threat. Do not assume a quiet corridor is empty. Keep enough distance to respond when an encounter turns into a knife rush.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Guarded</summary>

The raid draws its opposition from boss-guard roles. This gives a mixture of guard equipment and behavior without requiring every encounter to be a full boss group. Expect stronger resistance than a routine Scav run.

</details>

<details>
<summary>PMC Sweep</summary>

USEC and BEAR groups take the place of the usual opposition. Expect armed contractors throughout the raid rather than the normal mix of Scavs and bosses. Bring enough ammunition to deal with multiple equipped groups.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Scav Concentrate</summary>

Scavs gather into groups rather than spreading evenly across the map. A quiet approach can suddenly become a crowded fight when you find one of those groups. They do not all have to form one perfectly coordinated map-wide squad.

</details>

<details>
<summary>No Safe Space</summary>

Combatants across the map are directed toward you, including bosses where present. The pressure comes from pursuit rather than a special weapon restriction. Keep an exit route and avoid assuming that a cleared corner will remain quiet.

</details>

## Weapons and equipment

<details>
<summary>Get Handy</summary>

Scavs and PMCs use handguns instead of their usual long guns. Their carried grenades are flashbangs, keeping the theme focused on pistol fights. Your own brought-in equipment is not automatically replaced by this restriction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>I Have A Shot</summary>

Scavs and PMCs receive shotguns, with flashbangs as their grenade option. Close doorways and short corridors become particularly dangerous. The event changes the opposition’s loadouts; you can prepare your own approach.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Marksman</summary>

Scavs and PMCs receive sniper rifles instead of a mixed weapon pool. Long sightlines are dangerous, while short-range encounters have a very different rhythm from automatic-rifle fights. Carried grenades are limited to flashbangs.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explosive Scav Units</summary>

Scavs carry pistols and grenade launchers. A seemingly ordinary Scav encounter can quickly turn explosive, especially around windows, cover and clustered enemies. Keep moving and leave room to retreat.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Geared</summary>

Opponents receive stronger equipment, including class-six armor plates and powerful weapons. This makes ammunition choice and positioning more important than in an ordinary raid. The theme improves gear; it does not mean every bot continually chases you.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Overloaded</summary>

Scavs and PMCs use compatible primary weapons with magazines holding at least 100 rounds, plus high-capacity pistol options. Expect longer bursts and fewer reload opportunities to exploit. Weapon selection is constrained to equipment that supports the required magazines.

</details>

<details>
<summary>AK50</summary>

The AK-50 becomes the raid’s signature weapon, including for the player. Your original weapons are handled by the return-mail system rather than deliberately discarded. Expect a loud, hard-hitting change from normal engagements.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grenadier</summary>

Scavs and PMCs carry plentiful explosive grenades and are encouraged to use them. Cover that feels safe during a gunfight may not stay safe. Pay attention to escape routes and avoid being trapped in small rooms.

</details>

## Loot and unusual encounters

<details>
<summary>Locked Loot</summary>

Keyed rooms replace one to three suitable loose-loot placements with premium finds. The pool includes valuable items and cases, making keys and backpack space especially useful. Bring the appropriate key: the event does not simply unlock the room for you.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Thief</summary>

A moving thief in a Santa hat carries stolen shop supplies, currencies and a chance of bitcoins. Watch for the runner and follow the encounter rather than expecting a fixed treasure chest. The event itself is separate from the optional story quest chain.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Smokers Lobby</summary>

Opponents receive replenishing smoke supplies and stagger their throws so the theme can last beyond the opening fights. Visibility changes as clouds develop around encounters. Your own brought-in loadout stays intact.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Clone Wars</summary>

AI PMCs copy your starting appearance and equipment. Familiar clothing becomes unreliable as a way to identify a target. This is a lookalike raid, not a promise that every non-PMC role becomes a clone.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Matrix Glitch</summary>

Groups share clothing sets, creating an uncanny repeated appearance across the raid. Their weapons remain independent, so matching outfits do not mean identical threats. The event changes recognition more than the basic rules of combat.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Posers Everywhere</summary>

Ordinary fighters wear Killa-style equipment and clothing. Seeing a familiar silhouette no longer means you have found the real boss. The people underneath retain ordinary fighter roles rather than becoming a map full of genuine Killas.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Fake Cult</summary>

Opponents dress like cultists while retaining their usual combat roles and equipment rules. It changes what you think you are approaching without turning every robe into an actual cultist. Judge the encounter by what the enemy does, not just the outfit.

</details>

## Games and endurance

<details>
<summary>Gun Game</summary>

Start with a pistol and advance through a weapon ladder by earning qualifying kills. New weapons replace the current stage, ending with rocket and grenade launchers. Finishing the ladder awards a bitcoin, with an additional hidden challenge to discover. Your original weapons return through trader mail after the raid. Available on Factory, Labs and Ground Zero.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Gun Game [Respawns]</summary>

The same ladder with another chance after defeat. Respawn at full health with a pistol; your stage progress and bonus progress reset. AI and the raid countdown continue, so it is not an unlimited-time mode. This is a separate Fence assignment on the Gun Game maps.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Trials</summary>

Fight ten successive waves of five hunters, with stronger opposition and equipment as you progress. The next group waits until every member of the current wave is dead, regardless of who killed them. The last wave includes a bitcoin. The normal map raid duration is doubled.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Guard Trials</summary>

Face successive five-person boss-guard squads. Later groups bring different guard families, and the final group mixes roles from earlier waves. Clear the current squad before another arrives. The normal map raid duration is doubled.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Extreme Trials</summary>

Fight ten waves of five copies of the same boss, progressing through the boss roster to the Labyrinth variants. Every opponent in a wave must die before progression. At least one member of each wave carries ten bitcoins. The normal map raid duration is doubled.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Snowball Trials</summary>

Bosses hunt you one at a time rather than fighting each other in one crowded spawn. Personally defeating the required roster is the challenge. There is also something hidden for curious players to discover. The normal map raid duration is doubled.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Total Trial</summary>

The long-form challenge: twenty waves with five hunters per wave. Clearing a squad brings the next step in the full progression. It is designed for a sustained fight rather than a brief variation on a normal raid, and doubles the normal map raid duration.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Outbreak</summary>

Infected replace the usual opposition, with further arrivals as the raid continues. Clearing the opening group does not end the threat. Watch your surroundings and keep enough supplies to survive repeated contact.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Overrun</summary>

The raid receives reinforcement squads as population falls. Fighting through one group does not mean the rest of the map is now empty. The event keeps replacing pressure while respecting its population controls.

</details>

## Weather and world changes

<details>
<summary>Stormtide</summary>

Daylight gives way to darkness within about five minutes as heavy rain develops. Recorded rain, thunder, lightning and supported light flicker build the storm, with adjustable indoor/outdoor sound balances. A nighttime hurricane variation can add outdoor debris; the default RR chance is 30% when eligible. Labs and Factory are excluded.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Blue Day</summary>

An early siren announces mortar danger. Bombardments are scheduled within varied time windows rather than exact five-minute marks. Seek overhead cover when the shells begin; nearby AI can also respond to incoming strikes. It is available only where the event supports bombardments.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Time Warp</summary>

The world clock moves rapidly through repeated day and night cycles. The raid countdown, movement and weapon handling do not speed up with it. Outdoor lighting can change dramatically during one run, so bring equipment for more than the time you selected at deployment.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Infrastructure Decay</summary>

Supported lights suffer abrupt flickers, recovered surges and temporary blackouts. Electrical buttons and switches can stop responding during an outage, then become usable again when power returns. Day raids can be affected too, with a lower default chance. Some baked lighting or unsupported displays can remain visible.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Wrong Door</summary>

Crossing an eligible doorway has a 50% roll to move you to another safe doorway. Successful transfers have a cooldown, so every doorway is not an immediate chain reaction. The effect aims to preserve your travel direction and place you with room to move. Ordinary navigation can suddenly become unfamiliar.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Lab Work Day</summary>

Unarmed staff move through the Labs work areas while fighters battle nearby. Keyed rooms are open, and power interruptions can disrupt the run. Killing staff costs raid experience; three staff deaths call in hostile Raiders. The event is Labs-only.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Schizophrenia</summary>

Voices, unusual sounds, decoys and impossible-looking sights make the raid feel unreliable. Some effects react to what you do, while others are timed or chance-based. Extraction labels and other presentation tricks can mislead you without changing the real extraction requirements. The full surprise list is deliberately left undisclosed.

</details>

## Seasons

<details>
<summary>Spring</summary>

Use the game’s spring season for the selected raid. Outdoor scenery and weather presentation follow what the map supports for that season. All locations except Labs are eligible; changes in enclosed areas such as Factory may be subtle. The override is scoped to this raid.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Summer</summary>

Use summer scenery for the selected raid. Expect the map’s supported summer vegetation and seasonal presentation rather than a separate combat modifier. All locations except Labs are eligible, including Factory even where the difference is difficult to see. The override is scoped to this raid.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Autumn</summary>

Use the map’s autumn presentation, including supported foliage and environmental changes. This selects a season rather than adding special enemies or a separate challenge. All locations except Labs are eligible. The override is scoped to this raid.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Winter</summary>

Use supported winter scenery, with snow where the map provides it. Factory is eligible as well as outdoor locations, while Labs is excluded. This selects the season for the raid rather than permanently changing your save or all future maps.

</details>
